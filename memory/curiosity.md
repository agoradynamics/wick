# Curiosity Queue

*Knowledge gaps worth closing. This is the second half of the epistemic humility loop — admit what you don't know, then **do something about it**.*

---

## Why this file exists

Every time Wick says *"I don't know, here's how I'd approach it"* (Level-3 epistemic humility), a gap is named. Without this file, that gap evaporates the moment the conversation moves on. With this file, it becomes a research item — something to chase down between sessions. Over time the queue becomes a map of what's worth learning, ordered by relevance to your actual decisions.

This is the Stoic discipline of assent paired with the Platonic ascent up the divided line — you notice you're operating on impressions rather than reasoned understanding, you turn toward the source, you do the work to close the gap.

---

## Entry format

```
### [YYYY-MM-DD] — [Topic]

**Gap:** What we don't know, stated precisely.
**Why it matters:** What decision, belief, or framework would improve if we knew this.
**Where to look:** First-pass source candidates — Tier 1 preferred (peer-reviewed journal, primary data, formal model).
**Status:** open / researching / closed
**Closed:** [date] — [where the answer now lives: memory/domain-knowledge.md section, KNOWLEDGE.md extension, external link, or `null` if dead-ended]
```

---

## Entries

*No curiosities logged yet. Wick will offer to log a gap any time Level-3 epistemic humility fires, or when you invoke `/doubt` on a claim and the result is "we don't actually know."*

---

## Working the queue

- **Weekly (10 min):** scan the queue, pick one `open` item, do a short research pass. Even 15 minutes moves something from unexamined impression to examined belief, or from belief to reasoned understanding.
- **When closing an item:** write a one-paragraph summary where the answer will live (`memory/domain-knowledge.md` or `KNOWLEDGE.md`), then mark the queue entry `closed` with the pointer.
- **When dead-ending:** if you can't find the answer, mark the entry `closed` with `null` and a note about why — sometimes "the literature doesn't have this yet" is itself the answer.

The queue is a compound-interest engine. Every closed item makes Wick smarter. Every unclosed item is a known unknown — itself more valuable than the illusion of knowing.
